# Phase 6: The Backlash Cycle (Month 8)

**Trigger:** Sufficient mainstream awareness to generate contrarian takes

## The Pattern

Once a technology reaches mainstream awareness, outlets need new angles. The obvious angle is: "but what about the downsides?"

This is not cynicism. This is how journalism maintains relevance — by questioning consensus narratives. The consensus narrative by Month 8 will be "LICHEN good." Therefore someone will write "LICHEN bad?"

## Coverage Pattern

**Contrarian Tech Publication:**
> "LICHEN Is Great Until [Bad Group] Uses It"
> Lists hypothetical misuse scenarios
> Interviews security researcher who raises concerns
> Interviews us for response (which will be "technology is neutral")
> Concludes ambiguously

**Think Piece Publication:**
> "The Messy Politics of Mesh Networks"
> 1500+ words
> Quotes from 3-5 academics
> Historical parallels (Tor, encryption debates, ham radio in WWII)
> No conclusions reached
> Comments section is a war zone

**Publication That Wrote Positive Coverage Earlier:**
> "We Need to Talk About LICHEN"
> Reframes earlier enthusiasm as naive
> "When we first covered this, we didn't consider..."
> Features critics who did not appear in earlier piece

## The Concerns Raised

These will be raised regardless of evidence:

1. **Criminal coordination:** "Drug dealers / gangs could use this"
2. **Terrorism:** "What if terrorists use mesh networks to coordinate attacks"
3. **Harassment:** "Stalkers could track victims off-grid"
4. **Misinformation:** "Mesh networks can't be moderated"
5. **Regulatory evasion:** "This bypasses lawful intercept requirements"

Our responses will be:
- Criminals already have communication tools
- The threat model doesn't change significantly
- These concerns apply to all communication technology
- We cannot prevent all misuse without preventing all use

These responses will be quoted. They will not satisfy the concern-raisers.

## The "Both Sides" Treatment

Mainstream outlets will structure coverage as:

> [Paragraph about benefits]
> [Paragraph about concerns]
> [Quote from proponent]
> [Quote from critic]
> [Conclusion: "it's complicated"]

This structure gives equal weight to hypothetical concerns and demonstrated benefits. This is perceived as "balanced."

## Who Will Criticize

- Security researchers (legitimate technical concerns)
- Law enforcement (jurisdictional concerns)
- Telecom industry (competitive concerns framed as safety)
- Academics (career incentive to publish critical takes)
- Pundits (content generation requires contrarian angles)

## Who Will Defend

- Users (it works for them)
- Privacy advocates (encryption and decentralization are good)
- Disaster response community (proven utility)
- Amateur radio community (long history of this debate)
- Open source community (freedom to fork)

## Duration

The backlash cycle lasts 2-4 weeks of active coverage, then fades. The concerns raised will persist as "frequently mentioned objections" in all future coverage, but will not dominate.

## Our Best Response

Acknowledge legitimate concerns. Refuse to engage with hypotheticals. Point to actual usage data. Do not get defensive. Do not claim the technology is beyond criticism.

"We built a communication tool. Some people will misuse communication tools. We believe the benefits outweigh the risks. Others can disagree."
