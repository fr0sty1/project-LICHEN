# Phase 4: Tech Mainstream (Month 3-4)

**Trigger:** Disaster coverage creates "news peg" for tech publications

## The Pattern

Major tech and business publications do not cover niche open-source projects unless there is a "news peg" — a reason to write about it now rather than later. The disaster coverage provides this peg.

Editors will say: "Our readers have heard about this mesh thing. We should explain it."

## Coverage Pattern

**Prestige Tech Publication (Long-Form):**
> 3000+ words
> Historical context (Meshtastic, LoRaWAN, amateur radio)
> Technical explanation with diagrams
> Interviews with developers
> "What does this mean for the future" speculation
> Sidebar comparing commercial alternatives

**Business Publication:**
> Financial angle: market disruption, startup potential
> "[Commercial alternative]'s stock mentioned"
> "Runs on $30 hardware" as competitive threat framing
> Interview with venture capitalist who says something about "decentralization"

**General Interest Publication (Tech Section):**
> "The Little Radio Network That Could"
> Lifestyle framing: self-reliance, maker culture
> Accessible explanation, minimal jargon
> Quote from academic who studies mesh networks
> Photo of hardware that is not the actual hardware

**International Business Publication:**
> 800 words, dry tone
> Mentions both the technology and the regulatory ambiguity
> References at least one economist or political theorist
> Finds a way to relate it to broader trends

## Characteristic Elements

- Reporter actually tried the technology (rare but happens at prestige outlets)
- "I set up a mesh network in my apartment" first-person section
- Comparison to early internet/early cellular
- "This could be big" hedged with "but probably won't"
- Comments from skeptics given equal weight to proponents

## What They Will Get Right

- The basic value proposition
- The cost comparison
- The "no infrastructure" angle
- That real people actually use it

## What They Will Get Wrong

- Overstating current scale ("thousands of networks" when it's hundreds)
- Implying it replaces cell phones (it doesn't)
- Conflating different mesh technologies
- The timeline (will say "emerged this year" ignoring years of development)

## Questions They Will Ask Us

1. "How many users do you have?" (we won't know exactly)
2. "Is this legal?" (yes, with caveats they won't fully report)
3. "Could terrorists use this?" (yes, they could also use paper)
4. "What's your business model?" (there isn't one)
5. "Who funds this?" (nobody, which confuses them)

## The Mainstreaming Effect

After this phase, LICHEN will be Google-able by normal people. Search results will include explainers from recognized publications. The technology has "arrived" in the sense that it now exists in mainstream information space.

This also means: people who want to criticize it can now find it.
