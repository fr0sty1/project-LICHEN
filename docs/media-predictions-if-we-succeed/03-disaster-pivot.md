# Phase 3: The Disaster Pivot (Month 2)

**Trigger:** Natural disaster + cell infrastructure failure + LICHEN sighting

## The Pattern

Every year, hurricanes knock out cell towers in the southeastern United States. Every year, someone files a story about "how neighbors communicated when the phones went down." In 2027, that story will include LICHEN.

We do not wish for disasters. We observe that they happen on schedule.

## Coverage Pattern

**Cable News (All Networks):**
> "After the cell towers went down, neighbors communicated with $30 radios"
> Human interest segment, 2-3 minutes
> B-roll of someone holding hardware
> Interview with a photogenic user who is not a stereotypical "tech person"

**Local News (Affected Region):**
> "Local ham radio enthusiasts help coordinate rescue efforts"
> Will conflate LICHEN with traditional amateur radio
> Interview with emergency management official who says something noncommittal

**National Newspaper:**
> "Open-Source Mesh Networks Challenge Emergency Communication Status Quo"
> Business angle: comparison to commercial alternatives
> "[Cost] hardware" repeated multiple times
> Quote from emergency management academic

## Required Elements

For this coverage to happen, the following must be true:
1. LICHEN must actually be used during the event (we cannot control this)
2. Someone must tell a journalist about it (human interest requires humans)
3. The "found person" story from Burning Man must be Google-able (it sets precedent)

## Framing Variants by Outlet Ideology

**Outlet emphasizing self-reliance:**
> "Americans don't wait for government — they build their own networks"

**Outlet emphasizing community:**
> "Neighbors helping neighbors when infrastructure fails"

**Outlet emphasizing tech criticism:**
> "Why do we depend on fragile cell networks anyway?"

**Outlet emphasizing business:**
> "The $30 device that outperformed million-dollar cell towers"

All of these framings can describe the same event. The outlet's existing worldview determines which frame they apply.

## The Turning Point

Disaster coverage is the bridge from "niche tech thing" to "thing normal people might care about." After this phase, mainstream tech publications will feel obligated to cover LICHEN because it is now "news" rather than just "technology."

## What We Cannot Predict

- Which specific disaster (hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires all qualify)
- Which specific region
- Whether anyone dies (this changes the tone significantly)
- Whether emergency officials embrace or dismiss the technology

## What We Can Predict

- The structure of the coverage will match the templates above
- The "$30" number will appear in every headline
- Someone will ask "why isn't the government doing this"
- Someone will ask "what about bad actors" (this seeds Phase 6)
